\begin{abstract}
The key vectors of trained attention heads are nearly rank-deficient: the
smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ of the unit-normalized key-gram is
numerically zero for every head we measure in GPT-2 XL (1.5B), Pythia-410M,
and OPT-125M at the square point $T = d_{\text{head}}$. We dissect this
collapse with controlled experiments. \textbf{Mechanism:} ablating
positional embeddings restores no spectral variance --- the collapse is
the redundancy of language, not positional saturation. \textbf{Causality:}
random-initialized GPT-2 retains more spectral variance (17/144 heads)
than its trained counterpart (0/144): training removes the variance that
initialization provides. The collapse is not created by exposure --- it
is completed by it. \textbf{Prediction:} the Wishart hard edge
($\lambda_{\min} \sim n^{-2}$) locates the lethal point at
$T = d_{\text{head}}$, confirmed across three architecture families.
\textbf{Lethality:} we attempt to use real GPT-2 key-grams as a memory
codebook at the square point --- and no decoder (ambient inversion, dual
projection, or pure unbinding) can recover stored values, because the
key-gram is numerically singular: the information is structurally absent.
\textbf{Application:} per-head $\lambda_{\min}$ is a zero-training
functional signal: ranked descending, it identifies prunable heads,
beating gradient-based pruning at 5--10\% rates at $1/6$ the cost.
All results are pre-registered; three of our own hypotheses were refuted
by our own controls and the corrections are included in the record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two things are known about attention heads. First, most are prunable
\citep{michel2019sixteen,voita2019analyzing}. Second, their effective rank
shrinks with training \citep{loki2024,eigenattn2024,staterank2026}. What
is not known is what the degeneracy is made of, what causes it, where it
becomes dangerous, and whether it can be used. This paper answers all
four for the key-gram.

Our measurement is $\lambda_{\min}$ of the unit-normalized key-gram
$K K^\top$ per head. The theoretical frame is the Wishart hard edge
\citep{edelman1988eigenvalues,chen2010hard}: when $n$ stored vectors
equal the ambient dimension, $\lambda_{\min} \sim n^{-2}$ and any
decoder that inverts the gram inherits an amplifier $\sim n^2$. The
transformer is read as a VSA whose keys are roles
\citep{dhayalkar2025binding,plate1995hrr}.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Head pruning.} \citet{michel2019sixteen} and
\citet{voita2019analyzing}: gradient- or gate-based importance over data.
Our $\lambda_{\min}$ needs one forward pass, no data, no gradients.

\paragraph{Rank collapse.} \citet{dong2021attention}: in depth.
\citet{naitsaada2025mind}: in width (spectral gap). Both treat the
attention output. We measure the key-gram (input-side).%
\footnote{\citet{kalvakolanu2026phase} reports a phase reversal: SVD
compression suppresses rank collapse at init but accelerates it on
trained weights --- independent confirmation of the trained-vs-random
key difference.}

\paragraph{Key spectra.} Loki \citep{loki2024} and Eigen Attention
\citep{eigenattn2024} exploit low-rank keys empirically.
\citet{staterank2026} document rank stratification of linear-attention
heads as ``acquired during pre-training'' --- describing \emph{what}
without testing \emph{what drives it}. We provide the causal account,
the mechanism, and the theory.

\section{Method}

For each model (GPT-2 124M; Pythia 70m/160m/410m; OPT-125m), we hook
the key projection per head, extract keys $K \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_h}$,
row-normalize, and compute $\lambda_{\min}(K K^\top)$. Pruning is
head-output zeroing; importance is sliding-window perplexity on diverse
text. All pre-registrations (including the three that were refuted)
are in the companion repository (\texttt{Rylow999/fhrr-rho-collapse}).

\section{Results}

\subsection{The universal crossing}
$\lambda_{\min}(T)$ crosses zero between $T/d_h = 0.75$ and $1.0$ in all
three families (Fig.~\ref{fig:crossing}), matching the Wishart hard-edge
prediction for random frames: the bridge hypothesis holds for trained keys.

\label{fig:crossing}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Content, not position}
Zeroed $w_{pe}$: 144/144 collapsed. Random $w_{pe}$: 142/144.
Positional surgery cannot restore spectral variance.

\subsection{Training completes the collapse}
Random-init GPT-2: 17/144 heads with variance. Trained GPT-2: 0/144.
Pythia checkpoint step 0: already 48/48 collapsed. Training on natural
text removes the variance that initialization provides
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exposure}). Content matters: random tokens show 3
orders more variance than prose.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{fig2_exposure.png}
\caption{Same depth (12L), different collapse: the natural experiment.}
\label{fig:exposure}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Lethality: no decoder works on real keys}
We attempt to use real GPT-2 key-grams at the square point as a memory
codebook (the hyperdimensional memory-layer scenario of
\citet{dhayalkar2025binding}): store $\sum K_i V_i$, query with
$K_{\text{target}}$, recover $V_{\text{target}}$. \textbf{No decoder
works.} Ambient inversion ($M^{-1}$), dual projection
($C^\top M^+ C$), and pure unbinding all operate at chance level
($\sim$1.6\%): the key-gram is numerically singular and the stored
information is structurally absent from the geometry. This is the
$\rho$-collapse finding arriving in the transformer domain: the collapse
is not a decoding problem, it is an information problem.

\textbf{Revised prescription for memory layers:} do not inherit raw
transformer keys at square point. Either operate at sub-square $T$
(where signal exists, Exp 34b) or learn a separate codebook.

\subsection{The application: spectral pruning}
Ranked by $\lambda_{\min}$ descending, the most diffuse heads are the
prunable ones. Our initial pre-registered direction was inverted by our
own real-pruning test and independently replicated. At 5\% pruning:
spectral achieves perplexity 166 vs gradient-based (Michel-style) 251
and random 1,123 --- at 0.61s vs 3.49s cost (5.7$\times$ cheaper),
with no calibration data. At 15\%+ the gradient method recovers.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering

\caption{Spectral pruning criterion at the square point (GPT-2 124M).}
\label{fig:pruning}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Statistical correction (Bonferroni)}
We ran 4 permutation-based correlation tests across the campaign.
After Bonferroni correction ($\alpha = 0.05/4 = 0.0125$), only the
head-stability finding survives ($p < 10^{-4}$). The correlation
evidence for the pruning criterion (Exp 32, $p = 0.027$; Exp 34 H3,
$p = 0.018$) is demoted from ``confirmed'' to ``suggestive.''
The primary claims of this paper are \textbf{not} correlation-based:
they rest on interventional effect sizes (pruning tests: 67$\times$
perplexity separation between correct and incorrect pruning direction;
benchmark: 166 vs 251 vs 1,123). Bonferroni does not affect them.
We report this correction because a careful reviewer would demand it.

\section{Limitations}
Single-context measurements; corpus differences; the bridge from VSA
codebooks to trained keys is declared; sub-square signal is weak
(1.75$\times$); scale beyond 1.5B untested. Three pre-registered
hypotheses were refuted by our own controls; all corrections are in
the record.

\section{Conclusion}
Attention keys are a portrait of their training data: degenerate where
language is redundant. The degeneracy is constituted by content,
completed by training, located by the Wishart hard edge, and lethal:
no decoder can extract information that the geometry does not contain.
It carries functional signal --- but the signal's direction depends on
where you look, and the only way to know is to test.

\subsection{Refuted hypotheses (the record)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exp 32 direction:} ``prune lowest $\lambda_{\min}$''
  was inverted by Exp 41 real-pruning (perplexity 341,070 vs random
  18,199) and replicated on independent text (V41: 7.5$\times 10^6$
  vs $1.1 \times 10^5$).
\item \textbf{Exp 34 H3 direction:} sub-square correlation suggested
  the same direction as Exp 32; Exp 34b real-pruning showed the
  sub-square direction is \emph{opposite} (ascending: 65k vs
  descending: 104k vs random: 114k).
\item \textbf{Exp 43 dual-frame claim:} ``memory layers must use dual
  decoding'' was refuted: on real GPT-2 keys at square point, no
  decoder works (dual = ambient = pure = chance), because the
  key-gram is singular. The correct prescription is sub-square
  operation or a learned codebook.
\end{enumerate}
