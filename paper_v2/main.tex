\begin{abstract}
The keys of trained attention heads are nearly rank-deficient: the smallest
eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ of the unit-normalized key-gram is numerically
zero for every head we measure in GPT-2 XL (1.5B) and Pythia-410M at the
square point $T = d_{\text{head}}$. We show that this collapse is governed
by \textbf{training exposure}, not architectural depth: Pythia-160m (12
layers, $\sim$84B tokens) is fully collapsed where GPT-2 124M (12 layers,
$\sim$10B tokens) retains an order of magnitude of spectral variance. We
further isolate the mechanism with positional ablations: zeroing or
randomizing positional embeddings restores no variance, ruling out
positional saturation --- the collapse is the redundancy of the content
itself, learned faithfully. These findings carry two consequences. First, a
zero-training head-pruning criterion: in lightly trained models, per-head
$\lambda_{\min}$ at the square point separates vital from redundant heads
(50$\times$ median separation, 9/12 vs.\ 0/12 collapsed, Spearman 0.558);
in heavily trained models the same measurement saturates, but a
\emph{sub-square} measurement at $T = d_{\text{head}}/2$ restores the
criterion (Pearson 0.236, $p = 0.018$), because the collapse signature is
head-stable across context lengths ($r = 0.66$). Second, a design
constraint: any memory or retrieval layer that decodes by inverting the
key-gram inherits the collapse by construction in deployment-scale models;
the only defenses are decoder-side (dual projection or iterative cleanup)
or measurement-side (sub-square). All experiments are pre-registered with
falsifiable hypotheses; negative results are reported as negatives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two empirical regularities about attention heads have accumulated in the
literature without a causal account. The first is old: most heads are
prunable \citep{michel2019sixteen,voita2019analyzing}. The second is newer:
the effective rank of attention states and key matrices shrinks as models
are trained \citep{wirtz2015smallest,loki2024,eigenattn2024}, with recent
work describing a stable stratification of heads into low-rank and high-rank
camps acquired during pre-training \citep{staterank2026}. Neither line
answers: \emph{what drives the collapse --- the architecture, or the
training?} And if the training: is the degeneracy positional (a fixable
design artifact) or semantic (a property of the data)?

We answer both questions with a deliberately simple measurement: the
smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ of the unit-normalized key-gram
$K K^\top$ of each head, swept across context length $T$ in three
architecture families (GPT-2, Pythia/GPTNeoX, OPT) and across model scale.
The measurement is the VSA one of us introduced in the frame-dual
stability program\footnote{The companion repository
\texttt{Rylow999/fhrr-rho-collapse} contains all 38 experiments with
pre-registrations, data, and honest-audit labels.}: when the number of
stored roles equals the ambient dimension (the \emph{square point}
$\rho = n/d = 1$), the smallest eigenvalue of a random Gram matrix hits the
Wishart hard edge, $\lambda_{\min} \sim n^{-2}$
\citep{edelman1988eigenvalues,chen2010hard}, and any decoder that inverts
the gram inherits an amplifier $\sim n^2$ \citep{nieto2026fhrr}. The
transformer is, in this reading, a VSA whose keys are the roles
\citep{dhayalkar2025binding,plate1995hrr}. The question this paper asks is
what happens to that geometry \emph{after training}.

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The crossing is universal.} In all three families,
$\lambda_{\min}(T)$ crosses zero between $T/d_{\text{head}} = 0.75$ and
$1.0$ and remains rank-deficient beyond (Fig.~1): trained keys behave as
quasi-random frames at the square point, the bridge hypothesis of the
frame-dual program.
\item \textbf{Exposure, not depth.} Pythia-160m and GPT-2 124M share
architectural depth (12 layers) but differ in training exposure
($\sim$84B vs $\sim$10B tokens). The former is fully collapsed at the
square point; the latter retains variance. Same depth, different collapse:
the driver is the number of tokens seen.
\item \textbf{Content, not position.} Ablating positional embeddings
(zeroed and randomized controls) does not restore spectral variance: the
collapse is not positional saturation but the intrinsic redundancy of the
linguistic content.
\item \textbf{The criterion and its recipe.} Where variance survives, the
per-head $\lambda_{\min}$ is a zero-training pruning signal (50$\times$
separation); where it does not, the same measurement at sub-square context
restores the signal.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Head pruning.} \citet{michel2019sixteen} and
\citet{voita2019analyzing} established that most heads are removable, with
gradient- or gate-based importance scores. Our criterion differs in cost:
it needs no forward pass over data, only the keys of one context. It
complements, not replaces, task-aware scores.

\paragraph{Rank collapse.} \citet{dong2021attention} proved rank collapse
\emph{in depth} for pure attention; \citet{naitsaada2025mind} identified a
spectral gap as the cause of collapse \emph{in width}. Both treat the
attention \emph{output} matrix. We measure the \emph{key-gram} --- the
input-side geometry that retrieval and memory layers must invert --- and
we identify its cause (exposure) and mechanism (content redundancy).

\paragraph{Low-rank keys and compression.} Loki \citep{loki2024} and Eigen
Attention \citep{eigenattn2024} exploit the low-rank structure of keys for
KV-cache compression; \citet{staterank2026} describe a rank stratification
of linear-attention heads as an acquired property. Our contribution is the
causal dissection: a controlled depth-matched comparison isolating
exposure, positional ablations isolating content, and a VSA-theoretic
account (the Wishart hard edge) predicting \emph{where} the degeneracy
becomes lethal to retrieval: exactly at $T = d_{\text{head}}$.

\paragraph{VSA reading of attention.} \citet{dhayalkar2025binding} proposes
attention as soft binding and hyperdimensional memory layers. Our results
make a concrete demand on such designs: the memory-layer decoder must not
invert the key-gram at the square point; it must be dual or iterative
(resonator-style \citep{frady2020resonator}), because the keys it will
inherit from any trained model are already collapsed.

\section{Method}

For each model (GPT-2 124M/355M/1.5B; Pythia 70m/160m/410m; OPT-125m),
we hook the key projection of every attention layer, extract keys
$K \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_h}$ for one English context of length $T$,
row-normalize, and compute
$\lambda_{\min}(K K^\top)$ per head. Importance is the KL divergence of the
next-token distribution when a head's output slice is zeroed, following
the ablation protocol of \citet{michel2019sixteen}. Full pre-registrations
(including falsification criteria) accompany each experiment in the
companion repository.

\subsection{Exposure, not depth}
Pythia-160m (12L, The Pile) vs GPT-2 124M (12L, WebText): 144/144 heads
collapsed vs.\ an $\lambda_{\min}$ range spanning $[1.2\times 10^{-5},
1.8\times 10^{-2}]$. Pythia-70m (6L) is already fully collapsed ---
exposure dominates architecture even at the smallest scale we test.

\subsection{Content, not position}
Zeroing $w_{pe}$: 144/144 stay collapsed. Random $w_{pe}$ (norm-matched):
142/144. Positional surgery cannot restore spectral variance.

\subsection{The pruning criterion and its recipe}
Lightly trained (GPT-2 124M at square): bottom-12 importance heads have
mean $\lambda_{\min} = 1.1\times 10^{-4}$ vs $5.6\times 10^{-3}$ for the
top-12; 9/12 of the former are collapsed, 0/12 of the latter; Spearman
0.558. Heavily trained: all collapsed at square, but sub-square
measurement restores the ranking (Pearson 0.236, $p=0.018$; head-stable
signature $r = 0.66$ between $T/2$ and $T$).

\section{Limitations}
Single-context measurements; corpus and family differences beyond the
matched pair (The Pile vs WebText diversity); the bridge from VSA
codebooks to trained keys is an explicitly declared hypothesis; ablation
importance on one text. All are flagged in the repository with follow-up
designs.

\section{Conclusion}
The keys of trained attention are a faithful portrait of their training
data: degenerate where language is redundant. That degeneracy is
\emph{acquired by exposure} and \emph{constituted by content}, not built by
the architecture nor saturated by position. It can be measured for free,
predicted from the Wishart hard edge, and defended against --- but only
from the decoder side.
